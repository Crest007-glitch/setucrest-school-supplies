Plan the practicals before buying the equipment

Start with the classes taught, the teacher’s selected experiments and the largest practical batch. Then check usable stock, room services and recurring consumables. A well-chosen apparatus set should let students take observations reliably, while leaving enough time for setup, cleaning and safe storage.

This guide covers Class 9 standard Science, Class 10 Science and Classes 11–12 Physics, Chemistry and Biology for 2026–27. Class 9 advanced Science, primary/middle-school kits and individual investigatory projects need separate lists. CBSE’s current Class 9 practical programme differs from older combined Class 9–10 lists; use the correct year when reviewing an existing quotation.

CBSE publishes both practical syllabi and a composite-lab equipment SOP. The tables below translate practical needs into purchase categories; they are not an exhaustive official inventory or a certificate of compliance. Check the linked syllabus, applicable lab SOP and NCERT method before the department signs off its final quantities. Approve salt-analysis and special reagents separately

The senior chemistry syllabus includes a broad ion panel: lead, copper, aluminium, iron, manganese, nickel, zinc, cobalt, calcium, strontium, barium, magnesium and ammonium cations; carbonate, sulphide, sulphite, nitrate, nitrite, halide, sulphate, phosphate and acetate anions, with oxalate also included in Class 12. Teachers should specify the actual sample salts and confirmatory reagents for their chosen programme. A list of ions is not an instruction to stock every possible compound.

Functional-group tests, organic-element detection, organic preparations and some colloid methods can introduce higher-hazard reagents. Order these only against a teacher-approved method, quantities, SDS, extraction and waste plan. Treat the syllabus’s alternative preparations as choices. Do not automatically bundle arsenic-based colloids, concentrated reagents or every organic synthesis option into a general school kit. Sample school lab kits by grade and budget

These are planning bundles to help build an enquiry. They are not pre-priced products or a promise of stock. Keep safety provision and necessary practical coverage in every budget; reduce duplication and stage optional additions instead.

Classes 9–10: composite lab

Core: shared glassware, microscopes and mount materials, prepared slides, Class 9 motion/separation apparatus, Class 10 electrical and optical sets, and the approved basic reagents.

Expand: add working sets where queues prevent students taking their own readings. Keep separate replacement allowances for slides, paper and breakable glassware.

Class 11: subject foundations

Core: the selected mechanics/thermal/wave setups; standard-solution and titration equipment; microscopy, plant physiology and identification materials.

Expand: duplicate high-use instruments after confirming accuracy, accessories, usable existing stock and the lesson timetable.

Class 12: advanced practicals

Core: selected electrical/optical setups, redox and analysis apparatus, approved kinetics/electrochemistry sets, and biology genetics/ecology/reproduction materials.

Expand: additional simultaneous stations or project-specific instruments once the mandatory programme is covered.

Use the CBSE baseline, then calculate the shortfall

The composite-lab SOP gives minimum quantities for a batch of 40 students. Examples include 10 microscopes, 10 beakers of 100 mL, 5 beakers of 500 mL, 10 separating funnels and 4 prisms. These examples are only a small part of the official inventory; read the full document and check the requirements applicable to your school. CBSE Composite Science Laboratory SOP.

Quantity example: if ten groups need two suitable beakers each at the same time, the practical needs twenty available beakers. If eight are usable, the initial shortfall is twelve. Reconcile this calculation with the applicable baseline and add any department-approved spare allowance. Chemical quantities should come from the selected procedure, repetitions and shelf life, not the number of apparatus sets alone.

Compare total cost, not only the item price

Ask quotations to separate apparatus, accessories, consumables, delivery, taxes and any agreed installation or training. Compare the same specifications and pack contents. An inexpensive microscope without suitable optics, or a burette stand without clamps, can leave a practical unusable. Prices and delivery dates depend on the final requirement and should be confirmed in the written quotation.

Lab layout, safety and chemical storage

The CBSE composite-lab SOP calls for a minimum 600 sq ft laboratory, two unobstructed exits, secure storage, emergency equipment and supervision. Review the full SOP with the school’s facilities team; this article does not replace building or laboratory approval. CBSE Composite Science Laboratory SOP.

  • Plan visible work areas: keep teacher sightlines, accessible routes and exits clear; separate wet chemistry from dry electrical work and protect services from spills.
  • Arrange storage by compatibility: use the SDS to separate incompatible chemicals, secure restricted materials and distinguish chemicals from apparatus and personal belongings.
  • Prepare emergency controls: confirm suitable extraction, protective equipment, eyewash, fire response, first aid and a rehearsed reporting procedure before practical work begins.
  • Control practical work: use teacher-approved methods and supervision. No mouth pipetting, tasting or direct sniffing of reagents. Use protected low-voltage teaching circuits for student electrical activities.
  • Plan waste before the lesson: provide labelled compatible containers and an approved collection route; do not assume chemical waste belongs in the drain. Prefer prepared slides, models or virtual material for disease-agent study.

Exact controls depend on the chemical, quantity, apparatus and school procedures. The teacher should review the relevant NCERT method and current SDS before authorising a session. NCERT science laboratory manuals.

Procurement timeline: work backwards from the first practical

  1. Before seeking quotations: teachers approve the practical list; the lab assistant audits usable stock; the facilities team checks benches, services and storage.
  2. At enquiry: share item specifications, quantities, selected methods where relevant, receiving address and the required ready-for-use date. Ask for item-wise availability and lead times.
  3. Before placing the order: reconcile substitutions, kit contents, total charges, delivery responsibilities and any installation/training arrangements in writing.
  4. Before dispatch: confirm that the room is ready, a receiving person is available and documentation and chemical storage have been arranged.
  5. On receipt, before student use: count and inspect items, check readings and accessories, log chemicals and run a teacher-led trial of the selected practicals.

Leave time between receipt and the first lesson for shortages or unsuitable items to be resolved. Use supplier-confirmed dates for your schedule; there is no single lead time that applies to every laboratory order.
